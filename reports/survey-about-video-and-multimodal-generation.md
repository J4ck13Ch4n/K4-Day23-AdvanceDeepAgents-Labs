# Survey about Video and Multimodal Generation

## TL;DR
- Diffusion models have overtaken GANs as the dominant approach for video generation, offering major improvements in stability and quality [1][2].
- Transformer architectures and hybrid diffusion methods allow for longer and more coherent video generation, but computational overhead remains a challenge [3][1][2].
- Recent multimodal models, such as SkyReels V4 and AudioGen-Omni, enable synchronized generation of video, audio, and text, leveraging joint training and advanced attention mechanisms [4][5].
- Leading benchmarks focus on evaluating not only quality but also compositional safety, procedural task execution, and alignment in multimodal outputs [6][7].
- Open problems include scalability, efficiency, dataset bias, interpretability, reliable evaluation, and robust cross-modal reasoning [8][9][10].

## Background
Video and multimodal content generation has seen rapid progress, transitioning from GANs—which faced issues of instability and mode collapse—to diffusion models that provide superior fidelity and temporal consistency [2][1][8]. The rise of Transformer-based architectures has enabled longer-sequence modeling and multimodal integration, essential for next-generation applications across entertainment, healthcare, and robotics [3][1][9]. Multimodal generation now leverages large-scale joint training across video, audio, and textual modalities, supported by unified frameworks and increasingly sophisticated attention mechanisms [4][5][6]. However, challenges remain in maintaining temporal coherence, computational efficiency, and robust cross-modal alignment [8][9][10].

## Main Approaches and Models for Video Generation
Diffusion models represent the most significant shift in video generation methodology, with DDPMs and Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) now regarded as the leaders for stable training and high visual quality [1][2][11]. These models are characterized by their stable training dynamics and ability to generate high-fidelity, temporally consistent videos, outperforming GANs which often suffer from instability and mode collapse [2][1]. Practical benchmarking reveals improvements in denoising, temporal consistency, and output quality, and their adoption is accelerating across both academic and industrial contexts [2]. Transformer-based architectures—including video diffusion transformers (DiTs) and hybrid transformer-UNet models—enable longer-range temporal modeling and finer token-level control, which is critical for multimodal conditioning and long-form consistency [3][1][2]. Block retrieval strategies and sparse attention mechanisms now improve computational efficiency, making real-time and longer-sequence generation feasible [3].

Hybrid approaches, such as S2PD, combine serial and parallel diffusion processes to balance quality with computational efficiency, allowing the generation of videos that follow both physical laws and logical event sequences, a key requirement in domains such as robotics and simulation [12]. Recent advances also include phase-aware video architectures, like PAVG, which employ dual-branch networks for modeling distinct dynamics for solids and gases, using spatiotemporal cross-attention to capture realistic interactions and maintain phase-specific motion characteristics [13]. Physics-informed video generation, such as PAVG, preserves phase-specific realism, representing an important step in domain-specific video synthesis [13].

Exocentric-to-egocentric video generation models like LEGO bypass explicit depth lifting, relying instead on video diffusion mechanisms to synthesize unseen regions, enabling generation from novel viewpoints without artifact-inducing depth mapping [11]. Such approaches are crucial in applications like autonomous driving and immersive experiences. Classification of video generation methods now includes UNet-based, Transformer-based, VQ-based (discrete tokenization), and hybrid designs, targeting improved motion consistency and scalability for longer videos and intricate scenes [2]. Technical challenges that persist involve maintaining motion realism, computational efficiency, and the reliable extension to multimodal tasks including text, audio, and even 3D generation [2].
## State-of-the-Art Multimodal Generation Techniques
Unified multimodal models like SkyReels V4, AudioGen-Omni, and JavisDiT++ represent the forefront of synchronized modality generation, employing dual-stream designs, modality-specific mixture-of-experts, and temporal-aligned mechanisms for joint optimization across video, audio, speech, and even text [4][5][14]. SkyReels V4 in particular uses shared text encoding to facilitate advanced control and fidelity in multimodal outputs, effectively supporting editing and inpainting tasks within both video and audio domains [4]. AudioGen-Omni leverages joint training and attention mechanisms to synchronize audio with video content, exceeding previous methods in synchronization and output quality as measured by task-specific metrics [5]. JavisDiT++ applies direct preference optimization and mixture-of-experts for joint audio-video generation, pushing state-of-the-art benchmarks in synchronization and fidelity [14].

High-quality video-to-audio synthesis frameworks, such as those presented by Taming Multimodal Joint Training, emphasize the importance of large-scale paired training for achieving semantic alignment and visually synchronized audio outputs [15]. These multimodal systems are validated against leading benchmarks, demonstrating gains in both quality and synchronization of generated modalities.

Benchmarking and evaluation have become central themes in multimodal research. Deep-structured diagnostic frameworks like OmniCapBench target fine-grained captioning and coverage-localization probes, exposing limits in current audio-visual captioning models and supporting continuous reasoning tasks [6]. Multi2AV-Safety focuses on compositional safety benchmarking, evaluating risks associated with interacting multimodal and temporal dependencies, revealing substantial gaps in current safety guards [7]. Streaming panoramic and procedural models such as SPW-Nav and WorldGuide bring real-time generation and adaptive procedural task execution into scope, extending multimodal capabilities to embodied agent navigation and closed-loop goal-directed video synthesis [16][17].
## Key Challenges, Trends, and Open Problems
High computational requirements for transformer-based and diffusion models hinder scalability, particularly for real-time and long-sequence applications where quadratic complexity and resource constraints become critical [3][1][8]. Many architectures rely on curated datasets, leading to concerns over generalizability, bias, and poor domain adaptation, especially in high-stakes domains like healthcare [8][10]. GANs, although widely used, struggle with training instability, interpretability, and mode collapse in diverse multimodal scenarios [8]. Diffusion models achieve high-fidelity results but bring interpretability gaps and demand vast paired corpora for domain adaptation [8][9].

The challenge of robust multimodal integration is significant as current systems still struggle to ensure semantic alignment, synchronization, and logical consistency across video, audio, and text [8][9][15]. Trends include vision-language pretraining, large contrastive models, and hybrid fusion strategies, which enable more robust cross-modal alignment and reasoning, but safety and objective evaluation remain open problems [9][10]. Benchmarks such as Multi2AV-Safety expose compositional safety risks, while OmniCapBench reveals captioning performance limits in deep-structured multimodal tasks [7][6]. Emerging research priorities target scalable and generalizable multimodal architectures, objective benchmarking, provenance validation, bias mitigation, and safety guarantees across diverse tasks [9][10][7][8].

## Trends and open problems
Scalability and efficiency are central themes, as diffusion and transformer models drive innovation but increase computational demands for generating longer, higher-resolution content [3][2][8]. Sparse attention strategies and hybrid diffusion models, as implemented in PARK and S2PD, help mitigate these issues by balancing quality, computational complexity, and temporal consistency for longer sequences [3][12]. Interpretable multimodal integration and safety remain prominent concerns, with new benchmarks—such as Multi2AV-Safety and OmniCapBench—uncovering compositional and localization risks, and motivating research into hybrid approaches for more controllable and explainable generation [7][6][8].

Dataset bias and reliable evaluation frameworks are open issues, with substantial efforts underway to enhance vision-language pretraining, cross-modal alignment, and to develop robust objective metrics for benchmarking multimodal tasks [10][9][7]. Procedural and adaptive generation systems are expanding, as models like WorldGuide and SPW-Nav enable complex, goal-directed, and interactive generation for embodied AI and robotics applications [17][16]. The evolution of multimodal diffusion and transformer models now emphasizes not only output quality but also compositional safety, scalability, and domain adaptation, reflecting the growing integration of these systems in real-world, high-stakes environments such as healthcare and autonomous systems [15][8][17].
